# LLM integration and world interaction

Reviewed: 2026-11-10. See the [main loop](execution_pipeline_and_resource_orders.md) for autonomous rules and resource orders.

## LLM runtime boundaries

OpenAI, Anthropic, Ollama, and Gemini response readers use try-with-resources for both success and error responses, closing their streams after reading. API-type commands accept case-insensitive provider names, including Gemini.

`LLMService` wraps asynchronous requests. Player chat, identity generation, inner thoughts, memory compression, villager conversations, and structure generation each have call sites. Skills initiate villager social interaction, with local dialogue and request-failure fallbacks. Apply asynchronous results on the server thread after checking entities and context. Player chat enters through `ChatMessagePacket`, whose reply handler runs on the server thread; JSON replies are read by `LLMResponseParser` (goals through `LlmGoalCatalog`, see below), and `GoalParser`/`AgendaParser` remain the fallback for legacy `TODO:`/`REMEMBER:` lines.

## LLM-authored goals

`LlmGoalCatalog` is the single vocabulary of goals the LLM may write. Each entry holds both the prompt line (`PromptTemplates.GOAL_TYPE_DOCS` = `LlmGoalCatalog.promptDocs()`, used by chat, thoughts and dawn reflection) and the JSON reader, so documentation and parsing cannot drift. Types: `craft`, `acquire_item`, `cook` (→ `butcher_cook`), `stroll`, `ask`, `drop_item`, `pickup_item`, `move`, `goto`, `eat`, `socialize`, `converse`, `trade` (notice board), `harvest`/`plant`/`till`/`collect_crops`, and the composites `sequence` and `invite`. `rest`, `sleep` and `wander` are routine-only: they are not documented and an LLM request for them is dropped.

A player-chat reply is applied by `ChatReplyProcessor` (parse → intake → agendas → thought), shared by `ChatMessagePacket` and `/va ask|preview` ([commands](configuration_and_operation.md#optional-llm)); `LlmGoalIntake.process` reports each goal's outcome (accepted, or the rejection reason) and supports a dry run.

Pipeline: `LlmGoalCatalog.parse` (pure, any thread) → `GoalDraft` → `LlmGoalIntake.enqueue` on the server thread: `GoalDraft.bind` resolves villager names/ids through `KnownVillagers.resolve` and clock times through `GameClock`, then `GoalValidator` (recursive for `sequence` steps and `invite` plans), a cap of 10 unfinished LLM/player goals (routine goals are not counted) and `GoalQueue.add`. Chat, thought and dawn results and accepted invitations all use this one intake.

The chat state block and the thought prompt include the current in-game time and `KnownVillagers.promptBlock`: up to 12 loaded villagers within 128 blocks, nearest first, as name, profession, distance and UUID. `converse` accepts `"villager":"<name>"` (full or unique first name, nearest on ties; an unknown name rejects the goal); the legacy `target_kind`/`target_id` form still works.

Timing: any top-level goal (or an invite's activity) may carry `"at":"HH:MM"` (next occurrence; a time passed by under 30 s means now) or `"in_seconds":N` (at most one in-game day), and an end with `"until":"HH:MM"` or `"for_minutes":N` (in-game minutes; after the start, at most 6000 ticks; without a start it starts now). The end is `AgentGoal.endTick`, and the start-by deadline is capped at it. A single goal given an end is wrapped into a one-step `sequence` at parse time. Binding reuses `AgentGoal.scheduledTick`/`scheduledPriority` (raised to at least 7) and sets `holdUntilScheduled`, an `EXPIRING` lifecycle and a start-by `deadlineTick` 2400 ticks after the start. See [appointments](goal_utility_and_chance_factors.md#appointments) for scheduling.

`sequence` (`SequenceSkill`) runs up to 8 non-composite steps in order as child tasks of one root (`BehaviorExecutor.executeChild`). The step cursor lives on the goal, so an interruption or reload resumes at the current step; a plain sequence ends with the first step that does not succeed and the normal exit policy applies. An *activity* (`AgentGoal.isActivity()`: it has an end time or a `groupId`) holds together instead. In a group, after the walk to the meeting place (`move`/`goto`) it waits until every other participant is within 8 blocks or 1200 ticks pass (`group-gathered`). A WAITING step is retried after 40 ticks (without an end time at most 10 times). A failing step is retried 3 times, then skipped with a memory, and DELETE still ends it. With an end time, after the last step it lingers within 6 blocks of the meeting place, faces the others and starts a conversation with a nearby participant every 600 ticks. At the end tick it finishes wherever it is. Waiting keeps the watchdog clock fresh. Steps inherit the group, leader and end: `socialize` prefers a participant within 12 blocks (`SocializeSkill.preferredPartner`), and `stroll` (`StrollSkill`) lets the leader walk to random land positions within 10 blocks while the other participants follow the leader (within 3 blocks), until the end or 1200 ticks. Participation needs no registry: `GroupActivities.others` is every loaded villager holding an unfinished goal with the same `groupId`. `GoalCommitment` keeps a sequence committed through its steps' yield windows; only emergencies, curfew/exhaustion sleep and reactive combat interrupt it.

`invite` and `ask` both become an `invite` goal (`InviteSkill`) that stores invitee UUIDs and a plan (`AgentGoal` or sequence, usually timed) with a shared `groupId` and the proposer as `leaderId`. `AgentGoal.shared` tells them apart. For `invite` (shared) the proposer also gets a copy of the plan on the first acceptance, and guests follow the host on a `stroll`. For `ask` (not shared) only those who agree do the activity: the proposer gets no copy, and the invitee's decision prompt says the asker will not take part. Gathering happens only when the activity starts with a walk to a place, so an `ask` to cook is just the cook. Any top-level goal, invite or ask may carry a `"title"` (`AgentGoal.title`, persisted). `GoalText.title` shows it per viewer: "Dinner with Bram" (shared; names from `GroupActivities.participantNames`), "Cook meat (asked by Rowena)", "Invite Bram: Dinner" / "Ask Ann: Cook meat". The goals panel puts the title on the first line and the steps below it; `/va ask`, the thought prompt's goal list and invitation pitches use it too. An end time (`until`) is optional and the prompt reserves it for staying together for a while: without one a plan ends when its steps are done. A villager may have one unfinished `invite` and at most 2 unfinished group plans (`LlmGoalIntake.socialLimit`: `already-inviting`, `enough-group-plans`). Thoughts (`SOCIAL_PLANNING_HINT`) and dawn reflection suggest occasional invitations, and the known-villager list shows this villager's friendship with each. The walk counts as arrival when the `move` child succeeds or the block distance is within 3 blocks. The inviter walks to each invitee in turn with a `move` child that follows them; within 3 blocks `Invitations.propose` speaks the message (overheard by players within 24 blocks) and asks the invitee's LLM through `LLMGuard` for `{"accept","reply","thought"}` (journal kind `INVITE`). Without a configured LLM, or when the call or JSON fails, the invitee accepts if its relationship to the inviter is ≥ 0. On acceptance a `GoalNbt.copy` of the plan enters the invitee's goals through the intake, and the inviter gets its own copy once; both relationships rise by 2. Plans past their start-by deadline are not joined. An explicit (LLM/player) `eat` eats at least one item even when not hungry.

LLM- and player-sourced goals that are not completed persist in the villager's NBT (`Goals`, via `GoalNbt`: type, targets, timing, steps and cursor, invitees and plan); runtime state such as cooldowns, failures and task frames is not saved. Rule goals are re-seeded by routines and needs.

`AgentGoal` represents schedulable short-term goals. `LongTermAgenda` and its debt/deal/acquire/generic subclasses represent long-term commitments. Track commitment storage separately from executable goal generation; there is no complete generic loop for proactively decomposing agendas.

## Harness components

### Thought generation and persona representation

`VillagerEventHandler.onWorldTick` calls `VillagerAgentManager.tickThoughts`. This optional path defaults off, scans every 40 ticks, and uses a per-villager 6000-tick cooldown plus a nearby-player squared-distance check of 400. `requestThought` uses a fixed instruction template, current identity/mood/needs/activity/environment, and only the most recent memory. It does not select questions from a question bank. Its callback schedules goal parsing, memory writes, and broadcasts on the server thread. A parsed `GOAL:` directive still needs an executable handler and valid world conditions.

`VillagerAgentData.personality` is text, with eight local fallback descriptions and optional LLM identity generation. Hunger/fatigue are floats clamped to 0–100; mood is a five-value enum derived from needs and relationships. `GoalScheduler.utility` also reads personality text: English `friendly` or `cheerful` multiplies social utility by 1.5. This is keyword matching, not a continuous personality vector.

The social multiplier changes the score, not probability by a fixed percentage: ordinary selection normalizes against other feasible scores, and cooldown/partner/urgency rules still apply. Substring matching also matches `unfriendly`; Chinese synonyms do not match. Personality enters player dialogue, thoughts/greetings, villager conversation, and the `TradeSkill`-initiated negotiation prompts. It does not directly set the economic price table or implement a general risk/combat personality model. Relationship gains after dialogue use existing relationship thresholds, not LLM sentiment analysis. See the [class architecture and code walkthrough](defense-layouts/CODE_WALKTHROUGH.md) for the exact dependency map and excerpts.

Although `llm_temperature` is defined, the current OpenAI-compatible request body does not send it; the Gemini request does. Inspect each protocol's request construction before claiming a configuration value is effective.

`ModConfig` defaults to the `deepseek-chat` model identifier and DeepSeek's OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Defaults do not establish a running instance's effective model or the version behind an alias. Changing models can change identity text and proposed goals as well as language; unchanged rule execution therefore does not guarantee identical behavior. No cross-model quality measurements are established here. See [technical defense materials](defense-layouts/TECHNICAL_NOTES.md) for source excerpts and an explicitly unexecuted comparison/recording plan.

| Component | Existing responsibility |
|---|---|
| `LLMGuard` | Future wrapping, timeouts, failure counts, and circuit breaking; `queryText` for prompts that ask for text or a line protocol (greetings, villager dialogue, trade GIVE/WANT lines), `queryJson` for prompts that ask for a JSON object (player chat, harness decision, invitation answer) |
| `DecisionRequest` | Assemble profession, state, environment, goals, and an action menu |
| `DecisionSchema` | Parse action, target, and rationale |
| `ActionValidator` | Check action types and some item, recipe, and coordinate conditions |
| `DecisionJournal` | Optional JSONL traces |
| `HarnessDecisionPlanner.decideDailyGoal` | Method for requesting, validating, and registering a goal |

There are no external callers of `decideDailyGoal` in the current source. The `harness_drive_daily_goal` setting exists but does not establish a main-loop call. `LLMGuard` is used on specific paths such as villager conversation. Pick `queryText` or `queryJson` by what the prompt asks for: JSON mode on a text prompt makes the model wrap the text in an invented object (`{"lines":[...]}`, `{"spoken":...}`) that text parsers show verbatim; inspect timeout/thread behavior separately for each direct `queryLLM` path. The journal records decisions. Complete offline replay, goal-proposal DTOs, and a retrieval-based memory system are outside the current loop.

Passing action-schema validation still requires skill eligibility, current world-state checks, and completion evidence. Supported behavior is defined by `BehaviorExecutor` mappings and handlers. Building has separate command and execution entry points.

## Individual blocks and structures

`BlockInteractionAction` provides standing-position selection, approach, placement, and breaking. Breaking uses a FakePlayer context, suitable-tool checks, tool speed, per-tick progress, cracks/swings, real drops, durability, and Forge break events. Placement uses an item-use context, the Forge placement entry point, and real inventory consumption. Execution checks distance using settings such as `build_interact_range_sq`, whose default squared distance is 2.0.

`StructureBuilder` requests and parses structure JSON with name, anchor, and relative blocks. Anchors can express relative or absolute coordinates; blocks use registry IDs. `BuildOrderPlanner.validateStructure` resolves world coordinates, checks empty structures, overlap, and inventory, then computes placement order and returns errors, unreachable positions, and steps.

Ordering starts with candidates touching world blocks and expands through 26 neighbors. It prefers the lowest Y, then distance to the anchor, finds a standing position for each step, and maintains a simulated placed set. Blocks without standing positions or an ordering position become errors. Static standing checks and actual navigation cover different conditions; dynamic reachability of arbitrary connected structures requires in-game verification.

Validation can be returned to the LLM for bounded repair, controlled by `build_max_retries`. `BuildJob` stores steps, anchor, and cursor and provides NBT/world reconciliation methods; `tickBuilding` advances execution. Recovery differs from temporary resource-gathering plans: building data can be saved, while ordinary resource orders save demand and require a new claim; method-owned orders are transient and are recreated by acquisition.

`/va build place`, `/va build break`, and `/va build structure` are explicit entry points. See command completion and `VillagerAgentCommand` for arguments. `processGoals` yields the body to non-goal-driven actions; combat and explicit building have their own update paths.

Atomic interactions and structure requests are supported. Autonomous site selection, a complete construction supply chain, underground tunneling, scaffolding, and third-party mod interactions require separate implementation or verification. FakePlayer calculations alone do not prove full equivalence to real players for all enchantments, potion effects, or third-party behavior.
