# Capabilities, crafting, and economy

Reviewed: 2026-11-09. Behavior execution follows the [main loop](execution_pipeline_and_resource_orders.md); see [failure recovery](failure_recovery.md) for shortages.

## Capabilities and professions

Each agent's `CapabilityPool` contains FARMING, FISHING, SMITHING, BREWING, ENCHANTING, CRAFTING, GATHERING, SOCIAL, COMBAT, WOODCUTTING, MINING and QUARRYING. It stores XP and the last practice day, derives levels through `CapabilityLevelTable`, and is saved in agent NBT.

Every villager is born with one random hobby capability at level 1 (`VillagerAgentData.hobbyCapability`, fixed per UUID); nitwits have none. A villager whose hobby is WOODCUTTING, MINING/QUARRYING or FARMING is given a wooden axe, pickaxe or hoe once (`grantHobbyTools`, persisted flag `HobbyToolsGiven`, log `hobby-tool`) unless it already carries a tool of that kind; villagers from older saves receive it on their next tick. That tool category is protected like a profession tool, so it is never given away in a delivery.

Ordinary GATHERING, WOODCUTTING, MINING and QUARRYING keep separate stable NBT IDs. Existing GATHERING XP is preserved and is not copied into new skills. Logging/mining/quarrying require their own level ≥ 1; profession claim establishes WOODCUTTING 3 for lumberjacks and MINING 3 plus QUARRYING 3 for miners. `healProfessionCapability` also repairs these baselines during profession updates and deserialization; they are not merely a one-time experience bonus. Successful extraction awards configured practice XP to that specific skill. Picking up a loose item awards no production XP, preventing repeated drop/pickup training and buying ores from granting mining/smithing skill. Default recipe unlocks include basic axes for WOODCUTTING and pickaxes for MINING/QUARRYING. Existing external `craft_unlocks.json` overrides are preserved; add the new skill keys there if maintaining a custom unlock file.

Profession, capability, and resource role represent work identity, skill level, and resource-order eligibility. `ResourceProfession` caches resource-order eligibility and is synchronized from the actual registered villager profession. Lumberjack and miner have dedicated workstations/POIs (see [Workstations](workstations.md)); orders never assign an unemployed villager a profession. Initialization and profession-assignment capability updates enter through `VillagerAgentData` and `VillagerEventHandler`. Recipe eligibility follows actual capabilities and catalog queries.

Practice and exchanges update XP and dates through `addXp`. Daily slow updates apply decay, synchronize vanilla trade levels, and refresh unlocks. Decay has a two-day grace period, then multiplies current XP by `max(0, 1-(effectiveDays/rustDays)^2)`; `rustDays` defaults to 14. Entries without practice dates skip decay. Vanilla conversion uses `max(0,min(5,(level-1)/2))`; inspect `syncVanillaTradeLevel` for final entity writes.

`VillagerProficiency` / `ProficiencyCapability` are a separate Forge Capability XP interface. Start with `CapabilityPool` and actual callers when changing capability gates; inspect consumers of both models separately.

## Behavior eligibility and recipes

`BehaviorCatalog` supplies gating metadata, `BehaviorExecutor.TYPE_TO_SKILL` maps executable goals, and each skill's `canHandle` determines eligibility. Farming maintenance/reclamation are internal `FarmSkill` phases, with capability and tool checks in skill methods.

`DeterministicPlanGenerator` provides profession behavior-pool queries and the routine craft target. `RoutineScheduler` calls these to maintain candidates. Generation and Utility scoring are separate stages; runtime scoring is documented in the main-loop topic.

`SkillCraftCatalog` combines profession recipes, general recipes, and capability unlocks; `UnlockedRecipes` stores each villager's available entries. `craft_unlocks.json` configures unlocks. Craftability requires both unlocking and recipe resolution. Profession keys are normalised to the bare profession name (`villageragent:miner` and `minecraft:farmer` match villagers whose profession is `miner` / `farmer`). Every villager can craft a crafting table (`SkillCraftCatalog.ALWAYS_CRAFTABLE`). Built-in profession entries at the starting capability level include: lumberjack planks, sticks, wooden and stone axes; miner wooden and stone pickaxes, torches and bread; farmer bread, bone meal, wooden and stone hoes; toolsmith stone and iron axe, pickaxe, shovel and hoe, shears and furnace; weaponsmith iron sword, iron axe and furnace; armorer iron helmet, iron chestplate and furnace.

### Producible items

`VillageProducible` answers whether the village can ever make an item from what villagers gather themselves: logs, stone and ores (`ResourceCatalog`), crops, meat, white wool, fish and seeds, flint (fletcher gravel digging), feathers and leather (slaughtered livestock), string (fishing loot), iron/gold ingots by smelting, and any crafting recipe (vanilla or custom) whose every ingredient has such a source. Glass, paper and similar items have no source. Unproducible items are never routine craft targets or required tools, and failure recovery does not request them (`recovery-unproducible`). Daily supplies and starter items do not count as sources. `onlyMadeFrom(input, output)` is true when the input is not gathered, mined or smelted and every recipe for it uses the output (a hay bale only from wheat, a coal block only from coal); such inputs are never acquired for that output.

### Routine crafting

The REPEATABLE `craft` routine ("Craft goods at my workstation") exists only for professions whose behavior pool lists `craft` and whose stock table in `craft_targets.json` (see [configuration](configuration_and_operation.md#craft-stock-table)) has an item below its stock count. Defaults: miner 16 torches and 4 bread; every other profession, including the farmer, has no craft routine (a farmer bakes bread only as a `supply_request` answering a village food request). `chooseCraftTarget` walks the table in order and skips items that are stocked, not unlocked, without a recipe or not craftable by the village; an item whose ingredients are carried wins, otherwise the first one short of materials. Each run makes one recipe batch (`craftBatch`: 4 torches, 1 bread) capped by the shortfall, and `CraftSkill.chanceFactor` drops to zero once the stock target is reached. Missing ingredients are reported as `MISSING_ITEM` and acquired through failure recovery, which may post requests. While that `acquire_item` for the missing item exists, the blocked craft scores zero (`GoalScheduler.waitingOnAcquisition`) instead of re-failing every cooldown. The crafting table is never a stock item.

Items requested by other villagers are made by `supply_request` responses, and missing profession tools by the tool-acquisition goals of `VillagerEconomySystem`; neither uses the stock table.

For ordinary vanilla shaped/shapeless crafting-table recipes, `CraftSkill` uses `CraftingPlan`. It expands ingredients backward from a real inventory copy, searches recipes and tag alternatives, and plans dependencies by batch yield. Inputs are reserved slot by slot to prevent double-counting. Planning includes cycle detection, depth 24, 256 crafting steps, and a 4096 search budget. An ingredient that can only be crafted from the recipe's own output (`VillageProducible.onlyMadeFrom`: a hay bale in the wheat recipe, a coal block in the coal recipe, an iron block in the iron-ingot recipe) is taken from stock only: a carried bale is unpacked, but the planner never plans crafting one and never reports it missing, so a wheat shortage is reported as wheat. The recipe index is cached for at most 100 ticks, so datapack changes are reread within about five seconds.

With an unlocked wooden hoe and one log: one log → four planks; two planks → four sticks; the remaining two planks plus two sticks → one hoe, leaving two sticks. Here `craft` quantity means additional output, rounded up to whole batches; surplus remains in inventory. Steps execute separately using vanilla `matches`, `assemble`, and `getRemainingItems` to validate the grid, output, and containers such as buckets. All inputs and capacity are checked on an inventory copy before one commit; failure consumes nothing. Inventory changes abandon unexecuted steps and trigger replanning next time; completed intermediates remain.

Craftable ingredient recipes for unlocked targets are recursively added to the unlocked set, including tag alternatives but excluding raw ores without crafting-table recipes. Expansion runs during slow updates and crafting start, and is recomputed after capability refresh. Dependencies do not become independent random daily crafting goals; the current plan schedules them. `AcquireItemSkill` delegates crafting, extraction, smelting and village requests to the shared method executor with stall-time scoring; `RestockSkill` delegates to acquisition. Selected crafting reuses craft child tasks. Missing-hoe recovery prefers an unlocked iron/stone/wooden hoe craftable from current inventory; it neither unlocks wooden hoes automatically nor substitutes wood for iron.

Steps fitting 2×2 can be crafted by hand. Steps requiring 3×3 use a real `minecraft:crafting_table`, never a profession block such as a composter or carpenter table. `CraftSkill.findNearestWorkbench` takes the nearest loaded table from chunk memory (`findWorkbenches()`, `ChunkFeature.CRAFTING_TABLE`) within 128 blocks, else a table found by a direct scan within 8 blocks (3 vertically). With none, a carried table is placed beside the villager (`place_near_self`); without one, the craft runs an `acquire_item` child for a crafting table (usually crafted from planks in the 2×2 grid) and then places it. A usable table that turns up while the table is being acquired ends that child: an "acquire crafting table" acquisition counts as satisfied as soon as a usable table is near, and its crafting or request is cancelled. Crafting scores do not include the table. Toolsmiths and weaponsmiths receive a crafting table among their starter items. `WorkbenchMemory` is an empty compatibility stub.

`smelt` is an output-indexed acquisition method for iron/gold ingots (SMITHING >= 1). It recursively acquires one missing ore and, when needed, coal through the acquisition methods, walks to a reachable loaded furnace/blast furnace, loads real slots, waits for vanilla processing, and collects actual output. Each batch returns to inventory-based acquisition completion; missing suppliers, barter funds, capacity or reachable furnaces can still prevent completion. An existing matching batch/output is reused. Normal crafting only resolves CRAFTING recipes; cooking recipes cannot bypass real processing through `NativeRecipeResolver`. Other cooking outputs are not registered as acquisition methods.

A smelter that knows no furnace or blast furnace (chunk-memory `FURNACE` anchors within 128 blocks, or a furnace/blast furnace block within 24 blocks horizontally and 6 vertically) builds one: `SmeltSkill` places a carried furnace beside itself, or first runs an `acquire_item` child for `minecraft:furnace` (8 cobblestone, crafted by smiths). The smelt method's score then adds 2 plus the estimated cost of obtaining the furnace. A known furnace that is busy or incompatible is waited for, not replaced. Custom recipes retain their profession-station path.

Routine smelting chooses the input against each furnace's contents: existing ore permits only identical item/NBT stacks, while an empty input prefers gold ore over iron ore (the current supported set). A smelter loads a whole batch at once: every carried ore of that type, up to 16 (`FurnaceInventory.MAX_BATCH`) and the input slot's free space, so the furnace visibly holds ore, fuel and a growing ingot stack. Loading checks input and output compatibility/capacity for the batch before debiting stock. Fuel is topped up to one coal or charcoal per eight queued items (the burning fuel counts as one): existing fuel permits only identical item/NBT, empty fuel prefers coal then charcoal; no matching carried fuel leaves the existing stack unchanged. The smelter stays tethered near the furnace for the batch time (5 s per item in a blast furnace, 10 s in a furnace). `collect_smelt` takes finished ingots at any time while the rest of the batch keeps smelting; it is weighted 1.5 while a batch of the villager's is due and 0.2 otherwise. Empty slots never cause carried tools to be burned. Waiting does not top up on every tick. Collection selects reachable furnaces whose output actually fits the backpack, merges existing stacks first, transfers only accepted quantities and leaves the remainder in the output slot. There is no full-inventory ground-drop fallback. Furnaces are shared world inventories, without private batch ownership; another actor can take or change a batch. Interrupted batches remain in the world and can be rediscovered.

Custom recipes cover items without vanilla recipes and similar cases. Profession catalogs contain attributes such as master enchanting eligibility; execution reads them for equipment output. When adding items, check unlocks, vanilla/custom recipes, ingredient supply, and inventory changes on completion.

## Trade and supplies

Villager-to-villager supply is free and demand-driven (`CooperativeSupply`, `SupplyPolicy`, `CooperativeSupplySkill`). No price, payment, change or affordability check controls cooperation between villagers; the value tables below apply only to the isolated player barter path.

Players can right-click a physical notice board to inspect its village's current requests and shareable stock. Request rows show the exact item, a category (`Any food`, `Any pickaxe`) or an "or" request (`Stick (or 2 Oak Planks / 1 Oak Log)`), the requested units (food in hunger points), the author and the provider whose delivery is in progress. Non-exclusive production has no unique assigned producer and is not shown; see [notice-board interface](workstations.md#notice-board-player-interface).

### Task structure (HTN)

Cooperative supply is expressed as compound tasks, methods and one joint primitive. Tasks only report what they lack; how a missing item is obtained is decided solely by method selection inside `acquire_item`. Villagers decide from their own `BoardMemory`, a snapshot refreshed only when they physically read the board.

```
check_board (routine "trade", REPEATABLE, idle activity like wander, 4800-tick cooldown)
  read board (stand 60 ticks) → refresh BoardMemory
  ├─ drop own unclaimed supply_request tasks whose request is gone, expired or claimed by another
  ├─ claim offers for own OPEN requests that a neighbour's offer covers completely
  ├─ post "for sale" offers (TRADE_HAVE): only items of active_offers.json for the profession, quantity =
  │  shareable stock minus output promised to own supply_request tasks; own stale offers are removed
  └─ for each OPEN request of another resident (max 2 new per read):
       stock covers the whole request with one alternative (checked in order), or an alternative can be
       produced without requesting any item of this request → add supply_request

acquire_item(item | category, units)                      requester side
  methods: craft | craft_inputs | extract (farm / lumberjack_work / miner_work session) | smelt | request_supply
  request_supply
    0. adopt    this villager already has a live request covering the need → wait on it, no walk
    1. post     read board (60 ticks), post one request for the shortage (one per villager and spec);
                an offer matching it → request only what the offer covers and claim the offer at once
                (requester walks to the provider), the rest is asked for after that delivery
    2. await    request OPEN or its delivery meeting running; other methods may run meanwhile
    3. verify   after delivery: objective met → retire, DONE; an "or" alternative received in full → retire
                and fail this attempt so crafting converts it; else retire the consumed request and go to 1

supply_request(request)                                   provider side
  1. produce    acquisition executor for the chosen output; no acquisition under this root may request
                the request's item or any of its alternatives; until shareable stock covers the request
  2. claim      read board (60 ticks); not OPEN to me → leave, keep items; else OPEN → CLAIMED,
                book the meeting (both villagers get a meet_trade goal) → done

meet_trade (root goal per participant; TradeCoordinator session)
  claimed request: provider walks to requester → requester invited within 16 blocks
  claimed offer:   requester walks to provider → provider invited within 16 blocks
  → both walk together → provider throws → contact pickup
  ends on completion, death, unreachability, interruption, unload or timeout
```

**Meetings.** A claim makes the request CLAIMED, so no other villager can claim it. The provider gets a `meet_trade` goal (priority 8) and walks to the requester wherever it is. Once the provider is within 16 blocks and has a path that reaches the requester (or can see it), the requester gets its own `meet_trade` goal (`trade-invite`). The requester joins when its current task ends or reaches a yield point between work units — between two crops, trees or mined blocks (see [Goal utility](goal_utility_and_chance_factors.md#commitment-and-yield-points)); a runnable `meet_trade` is picked before any ordinary goal. Both then walk towards each other and the items are thrown when they stand within three blocks of each other with line of sight. Neither the requester's acquisition nor the provider's `supply_request` drives the meeting. Needed sleep, priority-9+ emergencies and combat still take over and cancel the session (`trade-meeting-cancel … reason=`). Operator-directed exchanges give both participants their goal at once and they also walk towards each other.

**Offers** ("for sale" notices, `TRADE_HAVE`). At each board visit a villager posts one notice per item it produces — the profession's list in `active_offers.json` (farmer crops and seeds, fisherman raw fish, miner coal and iron/gold ore, lumberjack logs, butcher raw/cooked meat, leather and feathers, shepherd wool, fletcher arrows, smiths their tools/armour/ingots, …); professions without a list (nitwits, unemployed) post none. The quantity is its live shareable stock (protected tools, own commitments and every not-yet-thrown reservation — claimed offers and claimed requests alike — are held back) minus what it plans to hand over for requests it has taken on; a notice whose item is no longer available is taken down. A villager about to post a request first looks for such an offer: it posts a request for what the offer covers (`CooperativeSupply.offerUnits`, checked against the provider's live stock, not the notice) and claims it at once (`claimOffer`, log `offer-claim`) — the same reservation and meeting as a claimed request, but the requester walks to the provider and the provider is invited once the requester is within 16 blocks. The claimed amount is subtracted from the notice. Because the reservation holds the provider's stock, the provider neither offers it again nor uses it to claim another request until it has more (e.g. a miner offering 3 iron ore whose offer was claimed sees an ore request and skips it, `supply-claim-skip … stock_short`). A villager's open request posted earlier is matched to a later offer on its next board visit (`claimOffersForOwnRequests`).

**Request lifecycle** (`VillageSignalBoard.RequestState`): OPEN → CLAIMED (provider claims the whole request at the board) → CONSUMED (its meeting ended); CLAIMED → OPEN only if the meeting could not be booked; OPEN → EXPIRED (TTL); CLAIMED → OPEN also when the booked meeting ends without a handoff. Spec never changes; an OPEN request's quantity may only grow when the same villager needs more of the same spec.

**Request ownership** (`OwnRequests`). A request belongs to the villager that posted it, not to the method attempt: a villager has at most one live request per spec (`postOwnedRequest` returns the existing one), and switching from `request_supply` to another method (e.g. a farm session) leaves the request up. Upkeep every 40 ticks retires delivered (CONSUMED, no method verifying it) and EXPIRED requests as bookkeeping. An OPEN request whose waiting tasks are all gone or whose objective is met by other means is taken down only physically: a `withdraw_request` goal walks to the board, reads it and retires it (`request-withdrawn`). A CLAIMED request is left to its meeting.

### Requests

A request is a `TRADE_WANT` message with a `SupplyPolicy.Spec`: an exact registry item, a configured category (`food`, `hoe`, `pickaxe`, `axe`, `shovel`, `sword`, `fishing_rod`, `shears`, `bow`, and the material categories `log` — any item in the logs tag — and `planks`), or an "or" list of alternatives, each an exact item or a category. An exact `minecraft:iron_pickaxe` request never accepts a different item. Wood is never asked for by species: a missing oak log is recovered as an "any log" acquisition (`requestCategory=log`) and posted as `#log`, and "or" requests list `#planks`/`#log` (e.g. `2 sticks | 2 #planks | 1 #log`).

"Or" requests (`RequestAlternatives.build`): when `request_supply` posts an exact shortage, it follows single-input crafting recipes the requester has unlocked, at most three steps down, and lists each input with batch-rounded quantities. Needing 2 sticks posts `2 sticks or 2 oak planks or 1 oak log` (key `minecraft:stick*2|minecraft:oak_planks*2|minecraft:oak_log*1`). Recipes with several different inputs add no alternative (a pickaxe needs sticks and iron), circular inputs (`onlyMadeFrom`) are excluded, and among several single-input recipes one whose input the village can produce is preferred (sticks from planks, not bamboo). Request units are units of the first alternative; any one alternative delivered in full covers the request, and alternatives are never mixed (`SupplyPolicy.units` takes the best single alternative, `select` picks the first alternative the stock covers). A requester holding a delivered alternative retires the request and lets its crafting method convert it (`supply-alternative-received`). `food` accepts any edible item without a harmful effect and is measured in vanilla nutrition (hunger) points; all other specs count items. Requests are posted only by `request_supply` and are owned by the villager (see Request ownership above). Their spec never changes after posting. The only state added later is a single claim. Hunger runs an `acquire_item` child of `eat` with the `food` category and the hunger points needed to reach 85; a missing work tool is a category requirement (any suitable tier). Skills that lack an input (e.g. `CraftSkill`) only report `MISSING_ITEM`; the owning method or `FailureRecoveryPlanner` turns that into an `acquire_item`, and `ResourceWorkSystem.request` only records such an objective.

An unclaimed request that expires fails the requester's attempt; the executor's cooldown and failure penalty bound retries. Consumed requests are hidden from readers until their owner retires them. While an `acquire_item` only waits on its open request it re-checks every 1200 ticks (`acquire-waiting … onRequest=true`).

### Responses

Villagers learn about requests only by reading the board into `BoardMemory`. A `supply_request` never reads its request remotely; the routine check or its own claim step reveals changes. A requester learns of a claim through its `meet_trade` goal. Stock is checked alternative by alternative in request order. Otherwise `CooperativeSupply.chooseOutput` takes the first candidate output (category outputs, or the alternatives in order) that `ItemTaskCatalog.canProduce` accepts (crafting, extraction or smelting with capability gates met) and whose estimated production cost is finite without asking the board for the request's item, any of its alternatives or the chosen output (`RequestAlternatives.forbidden`). A villager whose only route would post the same request again, or would ask the board for a work tool, skips it (`supply-skip … reason=only_by_requesting_it_or_a_tool`): a smith holding no logs ignores "sticks or planks or logs", an unemployed villager without an axe ignores a log request, while a lumberjack answers it by logging. Every acquisition nested under a `supply_request` carries the same no-tool-request rule (`Context.noToolRequests`). Inputs of a different item may be requested: a toolsmith answering a pickaxe request may request sticks and iron. The same forbidden set applies to every acquisition nested under the running `supply_request` (`AcquisitionTasks`). A villager keeps at most one response per request; different villagers may produce for the same request concurrently, and surplus is accepted. An unreachable or missing board only delays a check.

### Shareable stock and tool retention

Each profession protects exactly one best tool per required tool category. Categories are derived from the tool entries in `profession_essentials.json` (e.g. farmer → hoe, miner → pickaxe, shepherd → shears and axe, fletcher → bow and shovel). The best stack is chosen by `SupplyPolicy.tierRank` (wood/gold 1, stone 2, iron 3, diamond 4, netherite 5), then remaining durability, then lower inventory slot; NBT and damage are preserved and the selection is recomputed from live inventory, including immediately before throwing. Lower-tier duplicates and tools of other categories are shareable; a better received tool becomes the protected stack and the old one becomes shareable. No automatic upgrade demand is created.

Temporary commitments are separate from tool protection: own active `acquire_item`/`craft`/`smelt`/`extract_item` targets, outgoing reservations not yet thrown, and non-tool essential minimums are withheld. No additional permanent food/material reserve exists.

### Free delivery

A claim covers the whole request. `CooperativeSupply.startDelivery` requires the request to be open and unclaimed, selects concrete stacks covering its full quantity (items exactly; for food the delivered hunger points are at least the requested points, choosing the smallest item that finishes the requirement so only the final item may exceed), checks the requester's receiving capacity with `TradeInventory.plan`, books both participants in `TradeAppointments`, marks the claim and books the meeting session with a `meet_trade` goal for each participant (see Meetings above). A provider that cannot cover the whole request does not claim it. If the meeting cannot be arranged, the claim is undone atomically. Before throwing, the coordinator revalidates the claim, protected tools, shareable stock and capacity. The provider throws its stacks one way with the existing physical handoff (owner-reserved drops, contact pickup, 600-tick timeout); thrown items are never refunded or replayed. Received counts are recorded for diagnostics only. When the session ends for any reason, the claim is released and the request is consumed; the requester's `request_supply` verifies its real inventory and posts a new request if the objective is still unmet.

A `produce_order` goal is deleted on execution. Villager cooperation has no paid board matching, purchase baskets or `buy_product` method; `BarterExchange`/`BarterMath` are unused compatibility code, and the work-order book is compatibility data. Operator-directed `/villager` exchanges still use paired physical handoff with explicit terms.

### Values and bundle settlement

The four content tables `item_values.json`, `junk_values.json`, `payment_accepts.json`, and `active_offers.json` are populated; see [Economy value tables](economy_value_tables.md) for the numbers, layer precedence, and verification. No unknown item is implicitly assigned a nominal price: an item absent from all three value layers has no price, cannot be offered, and counts as zero when offered to a villager. Prices are cost-anchored, not balanced against vanilla trade offers.

- `item_values.json`: profession → item → positive finite subjective value; `default` is a shared fallback.
- `junk_values.json`: item → positive finite fallback value.
- `payment_accepts.json`: profession (or `default`) → item → `{priority, retain}`. Higher priority breaks equal valuation-ratio ties. Without an explicit rule, a profession-essential item with positive minimum is retained entirely; explicit `retain:false` still preserves its minimum quantity.
- `active_offers.json`: profession → items it produces; only these are posted as offers on the notice board (see Offers above).
- `price_modifiers.json`: `playerMargin` 1.1892 and `selfProduceDiscount` 1.0 — profession discounts are hand-authored into `item_values.json`, so the runtime must not discount a second time. `profession_essentials.json` and `item_sources.json` remain existing data.

Lookup precedence is personal table → profession → default → junk. Personal prices use one value for both buying and selling; player buying from the villager costs value × margin, and player selling receives value ÷ margin, so a buy-and-sell-back round trip costs exactly 2× at the current margin. `PriceVersion=2` invalidates old two-price NBT tables. Personal tables remain copies until reset; source-specific offsets/decay from the larger T0 plan are not implemented by this change.

A profession-essential with no explicit payment rule is retained and cannot be sold at all; `payment_accepts.json` gives every essential an explicit `retain: false` rule so surplus above the reserve stays tradable. See [Economy value tables](economy_value_tables.md).

The following barter rules describe the retained compatibility code and operator-directed terms; villager cooperation does not use them. `BarterMath` sorts eligible buyer inventory by seller-value / buyer-value descending, then explicit priority and stable item ID. It greedily takes integer quantities until the seller's valuation covers the ask. Both parties must price every payment/change item. Retained stock, essential minimums, tagged stacks and the requested goods on the buyer's side are excluded. Change uses the inverse valuation-ratio ordering and cannot reduce the seller's net receipt below the ask. If exact change is unavailable, remaining overpayment stays in the buyer's cost; there is no debt or free rounding.

Buyer acceptance probability is `clamp(2 - netPaidByBuyer / gainedByBuyer, 0, 1)`: equal or better value is certain, double cost is rejected. Nonfinite and missing values reject. The greedy integer algorithm is not a proof of global minimum cost. `AcquisitionPlanner` checks only crafting feasibility from the villager's own simulated inventory.

Delivery timing. The provider claims at the board, so the meeting is booked only when the provider physically stands there with the whole delivery in stock, and only if neither participant is asleep or under night curfew, socializing, demo-occupied or already booked. A claim that cannot be booked logs `supply-claim-skip reason=…` or `trade-book-failed reason=…`, and the provider retries no sooner than 600 ticks later. Booking adds `meet_trade` goals even when a goal list is at its soft cap of 10. Walking re-plans every 20 ticks towards the partner's current position, falling back to a reachable standing cell beside the partner when the partner itself stands somewhere unpathable (a ledge, a crop). A villager within 6 blocks of its partner with line of sight but no path holds position and waits; a farther villager gives up after ten failed searches in a row (`trade-meeting-no-path`). A meeting that ends before anything was thrown reopens the request (CLAIMED → OPEN, `request-reopened`): the requester keeps waiting on it and any provider may claim it again. The session ends without delivery when either participant sleeps, fights, takes a priority-9 emergency goal, leaves its running `meet_trade`, the requester does not join within 1800 ticks of its invitation, or the 4800-tick booking deadline passes (`trade-meeting-cancel` names the reason); the requester's method then verifies its inventory and posts again.

`TradeAppointments` exclusively books both participants of a free delivery or operator-directed exchange. `meet_trade` goals wait for both participants to finish/yield ordinary work. Before throwing, stock or capacity changes, death/unload, village mismatch, expiry or interruption cancel the meeting. Once both meet within three blocks and line of sight, `TradeInventory.plan` validates exact NBT, outgoing stock and final receiving capacity. The coordinator stages physical drops of the agreed payment and goods/change, retaining NBT, then commits only outgoing inventory debits. If any spawn is canceled, all staged drops are removed and neither inventory is debited. Incoming stock is never inserted by this settlement step.

Drops follow normal throw physics and are reserved for their intended recipient with an infinite pickup delay, which also prevents vanilla item merging. Both participants stop navigation and wait in place after throwing. After 15 ticks, the meeting explicitly invokes contact pickup only for its tracked drops whose owner UUID matches the recipient and whose bounding box intersects that recipient’s pickup box, even with passive pickup disabled. Neither participant chases drops outside contact range; those remain pending until contact or timeout. Only the quantity actually inserted is removed from the entity; partial pickup leaves the remainder. Both meeting goals remain until every tracked drop is collected, with a 600-tick timeout after throwing. Funds validation and settlement are skipped once thrown. Completion retires matched messages and releases the booking. On death, disappearance/unload, interruption or timeout after throwing, the attempt is retired without refund or retry; surviving drops regain ordinary pickup. Such interruption can leave an incomplete exchange, but does not create replacement stock. Market and command exchanges use this physical handoff.

Player trade selections remain proposal copies. Server validation checks exact NBT, stock, capacity and eight-block proximity, and rejects nonfinite requested values. Unknown offered items contribute zero; unknown requested items cannot be bought. The existing optional LLM player evaluation remains a separate operator-configured path.

### Runtime village detection

`VillageRegistry` persists vanilla VILLAGE origin chunk IDs, structure bounding boxes and resident UUID affiliations per dimension. It reads structure references/starts only from already loaded chunks; no locate search or generation is requested. This deliberately avoids `getStructureAt`, whose mapped implementation may load referenced origin chunks. Unknown referenced bounds are conservatively protected from logging until discovered.

All loaded residents are considered each 100-tick refresh. A new resident joins the containing structure, otherwise the nearest registered vanilla village. Existing affiliation survives trips outside its bounds and save/reload. If no original village has yet been discovered in the dimension, the resident remains pending with no board; no bell/POI or arbitrary position creates a village. Removing blocks does not remove the registry record. Market identity is the persistent origin chunk, while orders and appointments remain transient and are republished from live needs/stock after restart.

Trade and combat messages use one runtime board per village; only economic access requires the physical block. Combat help calls remain immediate. A global message-ID lookup supports receipt/cancellation only, not cross-village enumeration. Unloaded residents lose quotes at refresh; world unload clears runtime boards. `/va markets` displays origin IDs and cached loaded membership. Resource profession assignment grants no tools; the existing currency-grant flag is preserved, but this requested change does not add initial endowment issuance.

See [Configuration and operation](configuration_and_operation.md) and [verification](DEBUG_VERIFICATION.md).

## Acquiring profession workstations

`FindJobSiteSkill` directly drives vanilla `GatherPOITask` and `AssignProfessionTask`. Vanilla code handles POI candidates within 48 blocks, reachable paths, ticket reservations, candidate memories, and assignment. A visible free station already within vanilla assignment distance (`BlockPos.closerThan(v.position(), 2.0)`) is first atomically reserved through vanilla `take` and handed to the assignment task, without requiring a path inside an enclosed cell. Distant navigation uses the POI's valid approach distance and vanilla path, recording its endpoint as the standing position instead of repathing into a solid block. Interruptions release candidate tickets through vanilla `releasePoi`, retaining claimed JOB_SITE memories.

Employed villagers whose stations disappear regain find-station candidates. Loaded stations are checked against actual blocks and profession POI predicates; invalid memories are cleared. Unloaded stations are retained without force-loading or treating them as destroyed. Vanilla assignment and Brain refresh remain in use; the mod still owns the full Brain tick and does not enable a second activity scheduler. Lumberjacks and miners claim their registered custom POIs through the same vanilla task adapter; nitwits and children are excluded. Failures use common backoff.

Method-owned purchase wants bypass ordinary author/item deduplication and automatic rematching. Their owning method chooses the offer and removes only its own requests on cancellation. Cooperative extraction is a separate waiting method with no payment, not a workstation refill. See [Task methods](task_methods.md).

## Profession essentials

The packaged `data/villageragent/economy/profession_essentials.json` maps profession IDs to `{item, minQty}`. Tool entries define protected tool categories: economy scans queue one `acquire_item` goal (priority 6, subject to the ten-goal limit) with a category requirement only when the villager holds no tool of that category; any tier satisfies it. A tool item the village cannot produce (`VillageProducible`) is skipped. Because string counts as producible (fishing loot), a missing fishing rod or bow creates a goal; crafting it can request string from the village. The goal first tries the villager's own crafting (requests carry an own-production penalty, see [task methods](task_methods.md#score-and-clocks)) and only then posts a category request. Non-tool entries, if added, keep their minimum quantity out of shareable stock.

`JobStarterItems` gives items once when a villager takes a profession: lumberjack iron axe; miner iron pickaxe; farmer 32 wheat seeds, iron hoe, 16 bone meal; weaponsmith 8 iron ingots, 32 sticks, 8 coal, crafting table; toolsmith 16 iron ingots, 32 sticks, 8 coal, crafting table; librarian 32 paper, 16 leather, 8 ink sacs; cleric 16 redstone, 8 glowstone, 8 lapis lazuli; butcher 16 carrots, 16 wheat, 16 potatoes, 4 coal; armorer 8 iron ore, 8 coal; fisherman fishing rod, bucket; shepherd shears, 16 white wool, 16 wheat; fletcher iron shovel, bow; cartographer 32 paper, compass.

`DailySupplies` grants at the workstation once per day, capping each item at twice the daily amount: farmer 16 wheat seeds; lumberjack 8 oak logs; miner 16 cobblestone; armorer, toolsmith and weaponsmith 4 iron ingots and 4 coal each; butcher 4 coal, 4 wheat, 4 carrots, 4 potatoes; shepherd 4 wheat; fisherman 4 string; librarian and cartographer 8 paper; fletcher 8 flint and 8 sticks; leatherworker 4 leather. Grants are not requests and do not make an item producible.

Daily supplies are granted only at the current loaded JOB_SITE of the correct profession, within two blocks and with an unobstructed ray to the workstation. Restock uses a goal-owned move child to a reachable adjacent standing cell. Failed movement, a destroyed/unloaded/changed station, or an obstructed workstation never grants supplies or updates lastRestockTime. Actual accepted inventory quantity determines success; zero inserted items yields WAITING, while a positive grant plays the workstation sound once. There is no elapsed-walk-time grant fallback.

Ingots are not fixed smith reserves; ore is a conditional smelting prerequisite. Requesting finished ingots and ordinary grid recipes are alternatives to smelting, selected by the shared method executor.
