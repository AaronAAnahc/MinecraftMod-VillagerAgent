# Capabilities, crafting, and economy

Reviewed: 2026-10-27. Behavior execution follows the [main loop](execution_pipeline_and_resource_orders.md); see [failure recovery](failure_recovery.md) for shortages.

## Capabilities and professions

Each agent's `CapabilityPool` contains FARMING, FISHING, SMITHING, BREWING, ENCHANTING, CRAFTING, GATHERING, SOCIAL, COMBAT, WOODCUTTING, MINING and QUARRYING. It stores XP and the last practice day, derives levels through `CapabilityLevelTable`, and is saved in agent NBT.

Ordinary GATHERING, WOODCUTTING, MINING and QUARRYING keep separate stable NBT IDs. Existing GATHERING XP is preserved and is not copied into new skills. Logging/mining/quarrying require their own level ≥ 1; profession claim establishes WOODCUTTING 3 for lumberjacks and MINING 3 plus QUARRYING 3 for miners. `healProfessionCapability` also repairs these baselines during profession updates and deserialization; they are not merely a one-time experience bonus. Successful extraction awards configured practice XP to that specific skill. Picking up a loose item awards no production XP, preventing repeated drop/pickup training and buying ores from granting mining/smithing skill. Default recipe unlocks include basic axes for WOODCUTTING and pickaxes for MINING/QUARRYING. Existing external `craft_unlocks.json` overrides are preserved; add the new skill keys there if maintaining a custom unlock file.

Profession, capability, and resource role represent work identity, skill level, and resource-order eligibility. `ResourceProfession` caches resource-order eligibility and is synchronized from the actual registered villager profession. Lumberjack and miner have dedicated workstations/POIs (see [Workstations](workstations.md)); orders never assign an unemployed villager a profession. Initialization and profession-assignment capability updates enter through `VillagerAgentData` and `VillagerEventHandler`. Recipe eligibility follows actual capabilities and catalog queries.

Practice and exchanges update XP and dates through `addXp`. Daily slow updates apply decay, synchronize vanilla trade levels, and refresh unlocks. Decay has a two-day grace period, then multiplies current XP by `max(0, 1-(effectiveDays/rustDays)^2)`; `rustDays` defaults to 14. Entries without practice dates skip decay. Vanilla conversion uses `max(0,min(5,(level-1)/2))`; inspect `syncVanillaTradeLevel` for final entity writes.

`VillagerProficiency` / `ProficiencyCapability` are a separate Forge Capability XP interface. Start with `CapabilityPool` and actual callers when changing capability gates; inspect consumers of both models separately.

## Behavior eligibility and recipes

`BehaviorCatalog` supplies gating metadata, `BehaviorExecutor.TYPE_TO_SKILL` maps executable goals, and each skill's `canHandle` determines eligibility. Farming maintenance/reclamation are internal `FarmSkill` phases, with capability and tool checks in skill methods.

`DeterministicPlanGenerator` provides profession behavior-pool queries and crafting-goal selection. `RoutineScheduler` calls these to maintain candidates. Generation and Utility scoring are separate stages; runtime scoring is documented in the main-loop topic.

`SkillCraftCatalog` combines profession recipes, general recipes, and capability unlocks; `UnlockedRecipes` stores each villager's available entries. `craft_unlocks.json` configures unlocks. Craftability requires both unlocking and recipe resolution.

For ordinary vanilla shaped/shapeless crafting-table recipes, `CraftSkill` uses `CraftingPlan`. It expands ingredients backward from a real inventory copy, searches recipes and tag alternatives, and plans dependencies by batch yield. Inputs are reserved slot by slot to prevent double-counting. Planning includes cycle detection, depth 24, 256 crafting steps, and a 4096 search budget. The recipe index is cached for at most 100 ticks, so datapack changes are reread within about five seconds.

With an unlocked wooden hoe and one log: one log → four planks; two planks → four sticks; the remaining two planks plus two sticks → one hoe, leaving two sticks. Here `craft` quantity means additional output, rounded up to whole batches; surplus remains in inventory. Steps execute separately using vanilla `matches`, `assemble`, and `getRemainingItems` to validate the grid, output, and containers such as buckets. All inputs and capacity are checked on an inventory copy before one commit; failure consumes nothing. Inventory changes abandon unexecuted steps and trigger replanning next time; completed intermediates remain.

Craftable ingredient recipes for unlocked targets are recursively added to the unlocked set, including tag alternatives but excluding raw ores without crafting-table recipes. Expansion runs during slow updates and crafting start, and is recomputed after capability refresh. Dependencies do not become independent random daily crafting goals; the current plan schedules them. `AcquireItemSkill` delegates priced purchases, crafting and cooperative supply to the shared method executor with stall-time scoring; `RestockSkill` delegates to acquisition. Selected crafting reuses craft child tasks. Missing-hoe recovery prefers an unlocked iron/stone/wooden hoe craftable from current inventory; it neither unlocks wooden hoes automatically nor substitutes wood for iron.

Steps fitting 2×2 can be crafted by hand. Steps requiring 3×3 travel to a remembered real crafting table instead of treating profession blocks such as composters as tables. During slow updates, `WorkbenchMemory` observes visible tables within eight horizontal blocks and three vertically. It saves up to 64 exact, dimension-qualified positions in villager entity persistent NBT. Recall checks loading, block presence, 128-block range, and vanilla path reachability. Without a usable table, execution yields and waits; it neither finds a profession workstation nor builds a table automatically. Existing `findWorkbenches()` coarse anchors remain part of environment memory; this path uses exact records.

Smelting, other processing, and custom recipes retain the `NativeRecipeResolver` / `ResolvedRecipe` profession-station path. Furnace fuel and processing-time simulation are not implemented here; special dynamic recipes are outside recursive planning support.

Custom recipes cover items without vanilla recipes and similar cases. Profession catalogs contain attributes such as master enchanting eligibility; execution reads them for equipment output. When adding items, check unlocks, vanilla/custom recipes, ingredient supply, and inventory changes on completion.

## Trade and supplies

Personal value tables copy the `default` entries in packaged `economy/item_values.json`, then overlay the current profession's entries. Shared prices include emerald 10/10, cobblestone 0.75/1.5, logs 2/4, planks 0.5/1, sticks 0.25/0.5, iron ingots 2.5/5, wooden hoes 1.5/3, stone hoes 2/4 and iron hoes 5.5/11 (buy/sell). These are explicit defaults derived from material values, not a runtime recipe-price calculator; profession entries and personal prices take precedence. Existing personal tables are preserved until explicitly reset with `/va reset_values <villagers>`; resetting discards personal overrides and rebuilds a deep copy. Missing items still fall back to 0.01; unknown prices are excluded from acquisition quotes. See [failure recovery](failure_recovery.md) for the bounded method-cost selector.

- `TradeRequestPacket` handles player requests; `VillagerTradeScreen` and `TradeResultPacket` provide UI and results. Prices use item values and personal valuations; completion depends on server checks and real inventory transfer.
- Cursor and trade slots contain proposal copies; they never debit the player's actual inventory. Clearing, canceling, pressing Escape, or replacing the screen releases selections. Rejection leaves real items untouched and does not create refund copies. Closing after submission does not revoke the server request.
- Requested quantities are capped by the combined inventory/armor snapshot minus selections in both request slots, with per-slot stack limits and exact item/NBT matching. Player offers likewise reserve only available stock. Server checks remain authoritative if either inventory changes after the snapshot.
- `TradeInventory.plan` first debits both parties on copies, then inserts receipts. It rejects the entire transaction if stock, NBT, or either inventory's capacity fails. Successful plans are applied on the server thread and synchronized to the player. The trade does not immediately reselect equipment, avoiding overflow when displaced armor cannot fit. The villager must be alive in the player's dimension within eight blocks.
- `VillagerEconomySystem` advertises priced surplus inventory in the resident's market; it retains profession-essential minimum stock. Listing is background bookkeeping and has no SOCIAL gate. `RoutineScheduler` no longer seeds `trade`; explicitly requested LLM barter still uses `TradeSkill` and cannot take participants booked into a market meeting.
- Buyers compare local offers through `AcquisitionPlanner`, selecting affordable feasible baskets (bounded search, at most eight quotes); direct purchases accept the quoted ask. Ordinary priced demands can be accepted when the seller's ask is no greater than the buyer's bid; those trades settle at the posted bid. Prices are emeralds per item, rounded up once per transaction, and freeze on matching.
- `TradeAppointments` reserves both participants on the server thread at matching time. Each UUID has at most one pending/active appointment, regardless of buyer/seller role or how many requests it has posted. `TradeCoordinator` adds two EXPIRING `meet_trade` roots with one shared session ID. They may enter only after the previous root finishes or returns WAITING; they cannot displace normal work. One ready participant waits in its composite root; both ready flags are required before either walks to the fixed meeting point. Emergency interruption cancels the appointment.
- Meeting expiry, first-ready timeout (600 ticks), lost goal, unloaded/dead participant, market change or invalid funds/stock cancel the session and release both slots. At arrival within three blocks of each other and the meeting point, with line of sight, `TradeInventory.plan` checks both inventories on copies and commits the whole exchange on the server thread. Nothing transfers on failure. Inventory is revalidated, not escrowed: ongoing work or player trades can consume booked goods/funds and cancel the appointment. `deliver_trade` remains only as a handler that deletes legacy saved delivery roots.
- `VillageMarkets` owns a separate `VillageSignalBoard` for each runtime market, within each `ServerWorld`. Offer/demand enumeration and supply discovery use only this board/member list. A global message-ID map supports direct receipt lookup and cancellation, not cross-village order discovery. Cooperative `WorkOrderBook` orders retain their separate per-dimension semantics.

### Runtime village detection

Membership refreshes every 100 ticks from loaded managed villagers without loading chunks. An exact vanilla `MEETING_POINT` memory identifies a bell market. Without that memory, HOME, JOB_SITE, saved home bed, then current position provide an anchor assigned to the nearest existing center within 64 blocks; otherwise a fallback market is created. Different dimensions never share markets. Unloaded or migrated residents lose their old quotes; a world unload clears its boards and appointments. Market UUIDs and orders are runtime-only.

This detects inhabited loaded market groups, not generated village structure bounds or every village in a world. Distinct meeting points create distinct markets; close settlements without meeting memories can share a fallback market. `/va markets` shows current-dimension market IDs, centers, anchor type, cached loaded-member counts and order counts. A group is considered loaded when its cache contains loaded managed residents; the center chunk itself need not be loaded. Membership and unloaded-message cleanup may lag by up to 100 ticks, but matching still checks live entities.

- `InventoryTransfer` transfers only what fits; full inventories and partial deliveries retain untransferred items.
- `JobStarterItems` defines first-assignment supplies and restock needs. Restocking submits requests; first-time resource profession claims receive one basic tool; changing jobs does not reset that resource starter flag.

`RecipeRegistry`, `CraftingAction`, and `ActionExecutor` remain in the tree. Trace active goal crafting through `CraftSkill`, `SkillCraftCatalog`, and `NativeRecipeResolver`. Packet registration, class existence, and actual client sends are distinct evidence.

See [Configuration and operation](configuration_and_operation.md) for data and settings.

## Acquiring profession workstations

`FindJobSiteSkill` directly drives vanilla `GatherPOITask` and `AssignProfessionTask`. Vanilla code handles POI candidates within 48 blocks, reachable paths, ticket reservations, candidate memories, and assignment. A visible free station already within vanilla assignment distance (`BlockPos.closerThan(v.position(), 2.0)`) is first atomically reserved through vanilla `take` and handed to the assignment task, without requiring a path inside an enclosed cell. Distant navigation uses the POI's valid approach distance and vanilla path, recording its endpoint as the standing position instead of repathing into a solid block. Interruptions release candidate tickets through vanilla `releasePoi`, retaining claimed JOB_SITE memories.

Employed villagers whose stations disappear regain find-station candidates. Loaded stations are checked against actual blocks and profession POI predicates; invalid memories are cleared. Unloaded stations are retained without force-loading or treating them as destroyed. Vanilla assignment and Brain refresh remain in use; the mod still owns the full Brain tick and does not enable a second activity scheduler. Lumberjacks and miners claim their registered custom POIs through the same vanilla task adapter; nitwits and children are excluded. Failures use common backoff.

Method-owned purchase wants bypass ordinary author/item deduplication and automatic rematching. Their owning method chooses the offer and removes only its own requests on cancellation. Cooperative extraction is a separate waiting method with no payment, not a workstation refill. See [Task methods](task_methods.md).
